2. Prior work on test-time adaptation / calibration for chromatographic RT alignment
   Finding: no prior work found that updates the parameters of an alignment network at test time
   (test-time training or adaptation in the TTT/Tent sense) for GC-MS or LC-MS retention-time
   alignment. Deep-learning aligners found (DeepRTAlign, Nat. Commun. 2023; ChromAlignNet,
   J. Chromatogr. A 1604:460476, 2019) are trained once and applied without per-dataset updates.

   Related work the paper should cite, because it calibrates RT with a small set of shared
   anchor compounds (RT prediction, not alignment):
   - PredRet: J. Stanstrup, S. Neumann and U. Vrhovsek, "PredRet: Prediction of retention time by
     direct mapping between multiple chromatographic systems," Analytical Chemistry 87(18):9421-9428,
     2015, doi:10.1021/acs.analchem.5b02287. Fits monotone GAM projections between systems from
     compounds measured on both. VERIFIED.
   - "Times are changing but order matters: transferable prediction of small-molecule liquid
     chromatography retention times" (Bocker group, Jena; first author Fleming). Predicts a retention
     order index, then maps it to absolute RT with anchor compounds. Listed at
     nature.com/articles/s41592-026-03243-2 (Nature Methods, 2026) and as a chemRxiv preprint.
     NOT FULLY VERIFIED: full author list, volume and pages could not be confirmed. Check Crossref
     before citing.
   - Classical GC retention indices (Kovats; van den Dool and Kratz) calibrate RT against an n-alkane
     ladder, which is the standard anchor-based calibration in GC. Worth one sentence; not searched
     here, cite from the standard sources.

   Suggested wording: "To our knowledge, no prior work adapts an alignment network to a new batch at
   test time. Anchor-based calibration is established for retention-time prediction (PredRet; retention
   order index mapping) and, classically, through retention indices, but these map predicted or
   reference RTs through a fixed low-dimensional function rather than fine-tuning a learned dense warp."
